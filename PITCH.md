# OpenBookMap — Pitch

Three tiers, shortest to longest. Use the one that fits the moment.

---

## 10 seconds (the hook)

**OpenBookMap is OpenStreetMap for used and indie bookstores — a free,
public map built from phone photos of shelves.**

---

## 30 seconds (the elevator)

> Used and independent bookstores hold millions of books no one online
> can search. OpenBookMap turns a phone photo of any shelf into part
> of a free, public catalog of every independent bookstore — built on
> OpenStreetMap, with OCR that runs on the contributor's own device.
> No platform middlemen. The result is open civic infrastructure for
> the offline book economy: discoverable by anyone, owned by no one.

---

## 60 seconds (the meeting opener)

> You ever try to find a specific used book? You call three stores,
> drive past five more, and hope. Used and independent bookstores
> between them hold millions of books, often deeply curated — and
> none of that inventory is online anywhere a search engine can see.
> When a store closes, that curation disappears with it.
>
> OpenBookMap is a free, open-source project that lets anyone with
> a phone photograph a bookshelf, have the spines read on-device by
> OCR, confirm the titles, and add them to a public, searchable map
> of every independent bookstore in the world. The map data comes
> from OpenStreetMap; the inventory layer is what we contribute.
>
> It's deliberately tiny: a static site on GitHub Pages, no servers
> we run, every layer replaceable. The whole thing fits under a $20
> /month budget and is MIT-licensed. The point isn't to disrupt
> bookstores — it's to make them findable.
>
> Live at **openbookmap.org**. Source at
> **github.com/tautme/openbookmap**.

---

## Spinoffs for specific audiences

**For book lovers:**
*"Find any used book near you without making twenty phone calls."*

**For bookstore owners:**
*"Get your store and a snapshot of your shelves discoverable online,
without giving up your data to a platform. Contributors do the work;
you keep your sovereignty."*

**For the OSS / civic-tech community:**
*"OpenStreetMap for the offline book economy. Static site, on-device
ML, free tiers everywhere, every layer replaceable. Looking for
contributors and partner projects in the open-data ecosystem."*

**For funders or sponsors:**
*"Independent bookstores are closing at a steady rate; their
inventories are cultural memory that vanishes with them. We're
building durable, open digital infrastructure for that memory.
$0 to start, $20/month ceiling, MIT-licensed, no rent extraction."*
